# Roadmap

## Purpose

The Roadmap defines the strategic direction of the Secure Health Identity & Access System (SHIAS).

Unlike the detailed architecture and implementation documents, the roadmap focuses on **what** will be delivered, **when**, and **why**.

The roadmap supports Agile Scrum development by organizing work into product increments, releases, and high-level backlog items while remaining flexible enough to adapt to changing requirements.

---

# Roadmap Principles

The SHIAS roadmap follows these principles:

- Deliver working software early and frequently.
- Prioritize high-value features.
- Continuously incorporate stakeholder feedback.
- Keep releases small and incremental.
- Avoid unnecessary long-term commitments.
- Continuously refine the product backlog.

---

# Agile Methodology

SHIAS is developed using Scrum.

The development lifecycle consists of:

```
Product Vision
        │
        ▼
Product Roadmap
        │
        ▼
Product Backlog
        │
        ▼
Sprint Planning
        │
        ▼
Sprint Execution
        │
        ▼
Sprint Review
        │
        ▼
Sprint Retrospective
        │
        ▼
Potentially Shippable Increment
```

Each sprint produces a working increment of the platform.

# Planning Horizon

The roadmap is maintained across three planning horizons.

## Long-Term (12–24 Months)

Defines strategic product direction.

Examples:

- National deployment
- Interoperability improvements
- Mobile applications
- AI-assisted workflows

---

## Medium-Term (3–12 Months)

Focuses on planned product releases.

Examples:

- New modules
- Major platform enhancements
- Integration capabilities

---

## Short-Term (Current Release)

Focuses on:

- Sprint Goals
- Product Backlog
- Release Objectives

Short-term plans may change based on sprint reviews and stakeholder feedback.

---

# Roadmap Ownership

The roadmap is maintained collaboratively by:

- Product Owner
- Scrum Master
- Development Team
- Project Stakeholders

Feature prioritization remains the responsibility of the Product Owner.

---

# Updating the Roadmap

The roadmap is reviewed:

- At the end of every release
- During release planning
- After significant stakeholder feedback
- When project priorities change

Sprint planning does not modify the strategic roadmap directly; instead, it refines the Product Backlog.

---

# Definition of Done

Roadmap items are considered complete when:

- Functional requirements are implemented.
- Acceptance criteria are satisfied.
- Tests pass successfully.
- Documentation is updated.
- Product Owner approves the increment.
